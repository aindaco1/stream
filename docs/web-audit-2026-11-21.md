# Stream web audit

November 21, 2026 · Filmmaker workspace, public players and inline checkout · Store sandbox

The pilot’s public signup, film posters, program editor and checkout were tested together. The most consequential fixes were a mobile editor that squeezed film names to zero width, lost keyboard focus after checkout failed, and application files that browsers downloaded again on every visit. The playback follow-up below measures what changed for Shorts test and how much of the remaining wait comes from its opening film.

## Search and discovery

| Surface | Verified behavior | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home, FAQ and filmmaker agreement | A signed-out visitor gets a readable title, description, heading and server-rendered text. Home and FAQ have one same-origin redirect. | The pilot sends `X-Robots-Tag: noindex`. Decide which pages to index when a stable public origin is available. |
| Dashboard, settings and administration | Signed-out workspace and admin requests redirect to their respective sign-in pages. The authenticated workspace uses private, non-storable responses. | Keep account pages and recovery links out of search. Access control remains necessary for private information regardless of crawler behavior. |
| Film and program players | Direct URLs return the film or program title in HTML. A film description can use its saved synopsis only when the embed includes that field. The program lineup is assembled by JavaScript. Unknown titles return a real 404. | The sandbox players and screening room now consistently send `noindex`, including players containing sample credits. Field selections still govern what an embed reveals. |
| Filmmakers’ websites | The iframe works independently. Its markup does not add a heading or synopsis to the host page’s own HTML. | The FAQ now asks filmmakers to add the title and a short synopsis to the page where they paste their player. |

**At public launch**, choose the canonical HTTPS URLs for the home page and FAQ. Check those pages’ actual response headers from outside the network, include only intended indexable URLs in a sitemap, and verify them in Search Console. The pilot currently returns 404 for both `/robots.txt` and `/sitemap.xml`. Crawlers must be allowed to fetch a page to see its `noindex` directive. [Google’s indexing guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/block-indexing)

A filmmaker who wants their film found in video search can use a dedicated watch page with a prominent player, descriptive page title and accessible poster image. If Stream later offers video structured data for host sites, supply stable public player and poster URLs, honor the filmmaker’s visibility choices, and keep R2 paths and paid-media URLs private. Search appearance has to be verified on a public site. [Google’s video search guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/video)

Axe-core 4.13.0 checked WCAG A and AA rules through 2.2 and its best-practice rules. The 22 states covered signup, sign-in, FAQ, agreement, filmmaker and admin dashboards, expanded film and program cards and analytics, editor, Storage, Payments, screening room, both poster styles, checkout location/address, and Restore access. Each state ran in desktop Chromium light mode, 320 px Chromium dark mode, 320 px WebKit dark mode, and desktop Chromium with 200% text size. Keyboard checks covered skip-to-content, accordion and field-help controls, moving a film in screening order, entering and leaving checkout, recovery email entry and retry after payment-provider failure. ## Shorts test playback follow-up

The 4.49 s play above prompted a repeatable comparison. The measurement starts at the Play click and ends at the first displayed video frame in Helium. Each round restarted the pilot viewer, used the private R2 originals, and opened a fresh browser context for each title. Rounds alternated whether the program played first or after the three films. No media was requested in advance. Before the change we ran three rounds, and afterwards we ran five. The latter restarts retained only the saved technical inspection. All four previews were 10 seconds.

| Title | First frame before, median | First frame after, median |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Shorts test | 2.26 s | 1.15 s |
| Short projection chart | 1.11 s | 0.68 s |
| Subtitle screening test | 1.08 s | 0.71 s |
| Stream upload rehearsal | 1.36 s | 0.64 s |
| Shorts test, repeat play | 0.53 s | 0.11 s |

Shorts test opens **Intro**, a 1440 × 1064 High Profile H.264 file that Stream converts to a bounded preview. Two standalone films used the faster packet-copy path. The third was a 160 × 90 transcode. Before the change Stream fetched the start of Intro separately to identify the file, inspect its tracks and encode it. The browser also sought through the fragmented MP4 to establish its timeline, and each seek verified the original with R2.

New uploads now save a small record of their codec, tracks and duration during inspection. Existing titles acquire it on playback. Stream verifies the source revision and size before using that record, shares the first 128 KiB between readers on an inspection miss, and uses bounded 4 MiB R2 reads when encoding. The encoder copies the already-trimmed preview packets into a fast-start MP4 with the complete sample index at the front. In two Helium traces, this cut three video requests before the first frame to one. The faster preview encoder produced 3.35 MB instead of 3.24 MB for this intro. It kept the same dimensions, 24 fps and configured bitrate limit. On this clip, structural similarity to the scaled source was 0.984 versus 0.987. Paid encoding settings are independent.

- **Restart.** Shorts test ranged from 1.10 to 1.28 s across the five restarts. A separate first play with both the saved inspection and the media cache empty took 1.44 s and ended at exactly 10 s.
- **Same original.** Three alternating pairs opened Intro directly and through the program. Each used the same two metadata checks and two R2 range requests and produced the same 3,345,004-byte preview. Median server preparation was 1.19 s directly and 1.26 s through the program. No other lineup title was read.
- **WebKit.** A separate restart gave 1.42 s for the program and 0.36 s on repeat. Standalone films took 0.83–1.00 s. These browser measurements used a local viewer endpoint and remote R2.
- **Paid streaming.** The server now keeps up to 32 MiB or 128 recently rendered HLS segments across open films. Rewinding, retrying a segment, or another authorized viewer requesting the same rendition can reuse it. In a four-segment 960 × 540 fixture, median repeated segment preparation fell from 251 ms to 0.1 ms. A hit still checked the source and read zero video bytes. This measurement excludes network and player buffering. First-time segments still need rendering.
- **Access and retention.** Rental and purchase access checks still run for each request, including after rendering. Segment encryption happens at delivery. Different picture-caption renditions have separate entries, and segments with a visible personal watermark are excluded from the completed cache. The 96 MiB preview cache and the paid segment cache both clear on restart. The original stays in the filmmaker’s bucket.

## Public tunnel rehearsal

The replacement Cloudflare Quick Tunnel served the pilot over public HTTPS. A desktop Mac on the viewer host reached the public hostname through Cloudflare, with private originals in R2. Each preview round restarted the viewer service, opened a fresh browser context, and measured the Play click to the first displayed frame. Stream retained the saved codec inspection across restarts. Cloudflare returned `DYNAMIC` and the video response specified `private, no-store`.

| Public preview | First frame after restart | Repeat play |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Shorts test in Helium, 10 restarts | 1.32 s median (1.20–1.58 s) | 0.21 s median |
| Short projection chart in Helium, same rounds | 0.82 s median | — |
| Shorts test in desktop WebKit, two restarts | 1.53 s and 1.66 s | 0.58 s and 0.65 s |

The rental test restored an existing paid Store sandbox rental. Its saved edition included Intro, all three films and Outro. Both browsers used the paid media routes throughout. The test sought forward and backward, selected English on the subtitled film, went back to it, and let a film finish to test automatic advance. The browser suppressed test analytics and bookmark writes. No new payment was necessary.

| Paid viewer action | Helium | Desktop WebKit |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Start rental | 2.34 s | 1.79 s |
| Seek to a displayed frame, four seeks | 0.58–0.96 s | 0.08–1.08 s |
| Next or Previous to a displayed frame, six switches | 1.03 s median (0.92–2.64 s) | 1.34 s median (0.73–1.82 s) |
| End of one title to first frame of the next | 0.90 s | 0.72 s |

Turning on captions and immediately pressing Next exposed a player bug. While the browser loaded the caption rendition its video element temporarily reported a paused state, which caused the new title to stay at 0:00. Stream now remembers whether playback should resume while a title or caption rendition is loading. Public retests covered both a playing viewer and one who explicitly paused. English returned when revisiting the subtitled film.

Each browser contributed about 64 seconds of measured playback after actions. Helium recorded no buffering events within these intervals, and its largest observed frame gap was 83 ms. WebKit showed frame gaps up to 801 ms around title changes. A second 53-second WebKit run located the gaps. The first frame of each of the three films held for 484, 800 and 517 ms respectively. No later gap in those intervals reached 250 ms. Each seek resumed and both browsers completed every tested transition. Two optional lineup artwork requests on the older rental edition returned 404. All paid media requests in the completed runs succeeded.

## Priorities before a public release

1. **Measure mobile playback.** Repeat the public test on a physical phone over cellular, with a longer, higher-bitrate film. Record first-frame times, title-change delays and sustained playback stalls.
2. **Verify the checkout journey with assistive technology.** On an iPhone with VoiceOver and Android with TalkBack, try signup, a rental, a declined card, recovery, and program navigation with captions. Include the actual provider fields and the system video controls.
3. **Set the public indexing policy and test a filmmaker’s watch page.** Verify the intended canonical, searchable text, poster response, and embed visibility on that site. Keep drafts, orders and private URLs out of the sitemap.
4. **Measure the two remaining large repeat costs.** The program’s main poster and its first film’s lineup thumbnail each transferred about 54 KiB, even when they used the same artwork. Signup transferred about 2 MiB including the managed security check. Test smaller lineup thumbnails and measure real mobile signup completion before changing when the widget loads.
